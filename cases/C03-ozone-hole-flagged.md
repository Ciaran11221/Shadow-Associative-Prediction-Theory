# C03. The ozone hole, flagged as bad data

**What happened.** From late 1983, NASA's ozone satellite read Antarctic ozone below 180 units, where about 300 is normal. Its quality-control software flagged readings that low as possibly bad, and a ground station at the South Pole was reporting about 300, so the team doubted their own instrument. The satellite was right. The British Antarctic Survey published the ozone hole first, from ground measurements, in Nature in May 1985.

**What it shows.** An automatic flag plus a disagreeing reference made a real discovery look like an error. The reference can be the broken part (R5), and a disagreement between angles is a finding, not a reason to drop one of them (L2).
