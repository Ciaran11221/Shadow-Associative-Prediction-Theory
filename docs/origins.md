# Origins

Where the name and the ideas come from.

## The shadow: Jung

Carl Jung used "the shadow" for the parts of a person they hide or won't see. This theory borrows the word for the world: what official records hide or never measured. Jung supplied the word, not the evidence.

| Jung: the person | This theory: the world |
|---|---|
| The shadow: what a person hides or won't see | What official records hide or never measured |
| Read through indirect signs: dreams, slips, slow answers | Read through shadows: night-time light, lichens, steam |
| Not all bad: it also holds unused strengths | Not all lies: it also holds what nobody counted, like informal work |
| Interpreted by one analyst | Confirmed only when independent angles agree (L2) |
| Aim: make the hidden conscious | Aim: make the hidden measurable |

**Jung measured a shadow too.** His word-association test read hidden emotional "complexes" from slow answers, and later from skin readings on a galvanometer. In a 1934 lecture he described it picking out a thief among several staff. Its descendant, the polygraph, fails the shadow test: arousal moves with lying, but also with fear ([S13](../shadows/S13-arousal-for-lying.md)).

**The part not borrowed: synchronicity.** With the physicist Wolfgang Pauli (1952), Jung argued for synchronicity: coincidences that are meaningful without a cause. This theory does the opposite. Before a match counts, it checks how often the match would happen by chance, and whether something else caused both.

## Other roots

| Source | Idea | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Edmond Locard, forensic science | "Every contact leaves a trace" | L1 |
| Plato's cave | Prisoners see only shadows on a wall, never the things casting them | The metaphor |
| Shannon, 1948 | Borrowed "entropy" from physics and gave it a new, exact meaning | How to borrow a word and make it your own |
| Karl Popper | A claim that can't be proven wrong isn't science | Every law has a "false if" |

## "Shadow" in other fields

| Field | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Statistics | Shadow variable | A variable that tracks a value that's missing because of what it is, but plays no part in it going missing |
| Economics | Shadow economy | Activity hidden from official figures, estimated with shadows such as electricity use ([S11](../shadows/S11-electricity-hidden-economy.md)) |
| Optimisation | Shadow price | The hidden value of a scarce resource: how much the result improves with one more unit of it |
